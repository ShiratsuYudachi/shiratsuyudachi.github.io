LANG1404 · Paraphrase Court

The law

Three checks for a fair paraphrase

1 · Words

Mostly your own vocabulary. Keep only key terms that have no good substitute.

2 · Structure

Build a new sentence. Swapping synonyms into their sentence frame is not enough.

3 · Credit

New words, same idea — so it is still theirs. Cite it, in correct APA 7 format.

Same meaning + new words + new structure + credit = a fair paraphrase

Check 3, in detail

APA 7 in-text citations: two styles

AuthorsParentheticalNarrative
1 author… (Chan, 2023).Chan (2023) argues …
2 authors… (Chan & Wong, 2023).Chan and Wong (2023) …
3+ authors… (Sparrow et al., 2011).Sparrow et al. (2011) …
Direct quote“…” (Sparrow et al., 2011, p. 776).Sparrow et al. (2011) wrote “…” (p. 776).

& inside brackets, and in your sentence.

3+ authors: first author + et al., from the very first citation.

Exhibit 1 · The verdict

B — Too close to the original

When people expect to have future access to information, they have lower rates of recall of the information itself and enhanced recall instead for where to access it (Sparrow et al., 2011).

Why

Every word is copied (underlined). A citation credits the idea; copied wording also needs quotation marks.

Fix

Quote it: “…” (Sparrow et al., 2011, p. 776) — or paraphrase it properly.

Exhibit 2 · The verdict

A — Acceptable

Sparrow et al. (2011) found that people who assume they can look something up later tend to remember where to find it rather than the information itself.

Why it passes

New words (underlined), a new sentence frame that starts with the authors, and the same meaning.

Citation

A correct narrative citation: et al. for three authors, year in brackets.

Exhibit 3 · The verdict

B — Too close: “patchwriting”

When people expect future access to information, they have lower recall of the information itself but better recall of where to access it (Sparrow et al., 2011).

Why

A few words dropped or swapped (“better” for “enhanced”), but the sentence frame is still theirs.

Fix

Close the source, explain the idea from memory, then check it against the original.

LANG1404 · Paraphrase Court · Fails check 2 (structure) — a citation does not fix it

Exhibit 4 · The verdict

D — APA format error

Expecting to search for information later, people tend to remember its location rather than its content (Sparrow, Liu, and Wegner, 2011).

Why

Great paraphrase, wrong format: 3+ authors needs et al., and brackets use &, not “and”.

Fix

… rather than its content (Sparrow et al., 2011).

LANG1404 · Paraphrase Court · Passes checks 1 and 2, fails the format part of check 3

Exhibit 5 · The verdict

C — No credit given

People who assume they can look something up later tend to remember where to find it rather than the information itself. [ no citation ]

Why

Same good wording as Exhibit 2, but the finding is still Sparrow et al.’s. Without a citation it reads as your idea.

Fix

Add (Sparrow et al., 2011) — or start with “Sparrow et al. (2011) found…”.

Debrief

What did the court learn?

A citation alone does not make copied words OK (Exhibits 1 and 3).

Your own words alone do not make the idea yours (Exhibit 5).

Details matter: et al. and & are part of giving credit (Exhibit 4).

The bigger picture

Academic integrity means being clear about whose words and whose ideas you are using. The same three checks apply to text from paraphrasing or AI tools: it can still be patchwriting, and the idea still needs a citation.
